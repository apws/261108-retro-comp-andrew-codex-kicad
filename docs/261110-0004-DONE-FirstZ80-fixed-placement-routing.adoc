== Result and method distinction

The link:261109-2317-PLAN-FirstZ80-fixed-placement-comparison.adoc[implementation plan] has produced three checked two-layer PCB outputs on the same historical FirstZ80 placement. All 63 footprints, rotations, pad geometries, the 160 x 100 mm outline and the 371 schematic pin assignments are preserved. No historical power or signal copper was imported into a new routing input.

The second completed output is explicitly an *octilinear hybrid*: a fresh orthogonal seed followed by checked 45-degree corner refinement. The direct eight-direction A* trials did not complete this board. They are retained as rejected experiments and must not be presented as successful independent routing. This change of approach is part of the result, not hidden behind the folder name.

Our own cleaned A3 schematic and separate 7805 extension are described in link:261110-0001-DONE-schematic-7805-and-readable-wiring.adoc[their schematic handover]. The FirstZ80 PCBs use Andrew's full historical circuit, rather than our smaller supply extension.

== Correct source, exact placement

Falken's saved Reddit PDF and WEBP led to https://www.reddit.com/r/homebrewcomputer/comments/19bka92/simple_homebrew_computer_for_beginners/[the FirstZ80 discussion] by Andrew Lynch, account `lynchaj`. The matching native files were recovered from https://github.com/lynchaj/duodyne/tree/66a5a63fd8338d12c115bc2bee5dff2f435afae4/%21Development/FirstZ80[January 2024 commit 66a5a63]. This is the old pictured board, rather than Andrew's current work.

The source's `FirstZ80.jpg` visually matches the saved WEBP. Its native footprint coordinates were used directly. The outline centre lines are (60,60) to (220,160) mm; the 160.1 x 100.1 mm stroked bounding box is not the board's nominal size. Complete coordinates are in link:../reports/firstz80-plan/placement.csv[the placement CSV] and the frozen link:../firstz80/checkpoint/checkpoint.json[checkpoint manifest].

The board uses an orderly column arrangement of upright DIPs: CPU, EEPROM, RAM, PPI and UART, with MAX232 at the upper right. Supply parts are grouped at the upper left, serial connectors at the right edge, the PPI IDC at the lower right and clocks/decode/reset below the main ICs. This differs from our earlier horizontal DIP arrangement. It is another example of placing functional groups neatly first and then making the routing fit.

Falken confirmed after reviewing the comparison that the historical PCB was also routed with FreeRouting, consistent with its supplied Specctra/FreeRouting-style `.rules` file. It is therefore labelled the historical FreeRouting result. Its exact engine version, settings and optimization history remain unestablished; it must not be treated as an equal-settings comparison with our optimization-disabled 2.5.0 run. Raw source bytes and attribution remain under link:../reference/firstz80/README.adoc[reference/firstz80].

== Circuit and connector discrepancies

The original Scherrer GIF does not include a 7805. Andrew's expanded FirstZ80 schematic and PCB both include it. The pictured board is therefore not just an alternative placement of our unchanged GIF-derived circuit.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Area |Difference from our earlier circuit
|Supply |U9 LM7805, D10 1N5819, J1 barrel input and parallel J4 terminal input, bulk capacitors and supply test points. Nominal input is 9 V; a thermal/current rating is not established by these routing checks.
|PPI connector |P1 is one right-angle 2x13 IDC connector carrying all three ports. It replaces our three separate 1x8 headers. K1 selects GND or VCC for P1 pin 25; pin 26 is GND.
|P1 mapping |Pins 1-16 alternate PC0/PA0 through PC7/PA7. Pins 17-24 are PB7, PB0, PB6, PB1, PB5, PB2, PB4, PB3. Pins 25/26 are selectable supply/GND.
|Serial |J3 is a male DE9 with different mounting geometry, using RX, TX, RTS and CTS. Our earlier female DE9 uses DTR/DSR. J2 adds a right-angle six-pin TTL header: GND, active-low RTS, VCC, RX, TX, active-low CTS.
|Memory |The reference uses 28C256 EEPROM. Its pin 1 is A14 and pin 27 is active-low WE; our 27C256 has VPP and A14 there. The reference also connects CE to RD and OE to its ROM-select net, while our earlier circuit uses the opposite CE/OE assignment. These historical choices were retained; this comparison does not verify in-circuit EEPROM programming.
|Other parts |Nine diagnostic LEDs and resistors, LS rather than our HCT gate specifications, a 10 uF rather than 1 uF reset capacitor, a DIP14 UART oscillator footprint, different UART strap/unused-pin choices, and no mounting holes in this snapshot. Reference numbers also differ.
|===

Exact net membership was compared by (reference,pin) sets before conversion. A bijection proved that 31 changed autogenerated net names were name-only differences, not circuit changes. Datasheet metadata was synchronized and embedded symbol/footprint artwork was snapshotted locally, avoiding substitutions with today's stock parts. The original files remain preserved.

== Routing work and limitations

Manhattan uses orthogonal A* with the earlier directional cost model and a native accelerator. Its third candidate completed the board. The accepted routing stage took 138.553 seconds; all three Manhattan trials totalled 461.444 seconds. Compiler startup and other setup are not a calibrated benchmark.

Ten direct octilinear A* candidates were incomplete, including changes of net priority, via cost and reserved future-pad escapes. Simply putting failed nets first moved the congestion elsewhere. None was promoted. A separate fresh orthogonal seed was then computed from the same empty checkpoint, in 136.928 seconds; the published Manhattan PCB was not loaded as its input. A native-DRC-gated pass replaced 160 eligible right-angle corners with 45-degree diagonals in 1.808 seconds. All proposed corners in that pass passed. This is a conservative routing/refinement pipeline with the seed's vias preserved, not a successful independent eight-direction router.

The full-board Manhattan and hybrid previews consequently look almost identical. Cuts per leg are 0.1 mm (73 corners), 0.2 mm (19), 0.3 mm (8), 0.4 mm (47) and 0.5 mm (13). The diagonals are short, and much of the orthogonal geometry remains. The total length reduction is only 0.264%, with no via reduction. The link:../output/firstz80/octilinear-corner-detail.png[enlarged native-track detail] shows one 0.5 mm cut on each leg, forming a 0.707 mm diagonal. This is a limited corner refinement; it must not be described as an independently optimized octilinear topology.

FreeRouting 2.5.0 used its official local MCP interface. The first job reported zero remaining connections but stalled during optimization and timed out without valid output. A second job with optimization disabled completed in 138.278 seconds. The transport worked; these observations do not establish a general MCP-versus-CLI algorithm difference. No CLI control was run for this new comparison.

Native KiCad then caught a mounting-hole clearance discrepancy that FreeRouting had not reported: one GND track was 0.2102 mm from U9's NPTH hole against the inherited 0.25 mm rule. A recorded 0.06 mm outward detour corrected it, adding 0.12 mm of copper and two track segments. This is a disclosed post-import correction; the native rule was not weakened. The raw SES, before-repair board, findings and repaired board are retained.

All three projects use the same native source rules: 0.25 mm signal tracks, 1.0 mm power tracks, 0.20 mm netclass clearance and 0.80/0.40 mm vias, with 0.25 mm hole clearance. The inherited project has no additional copper-edge minimum; grid routing uses a conservative 0.6 mm centre-line boundary mask, while FreeRouting was configured with a 0.2 mm copper-edge guard. These internal guards, search costs, priorities and the hybrid's shared seed strategy differ. This is an illustrative engineering comparison, not an equal-settings performance benchmark.

The inherited schematic retains 47 warnings: 42 unconnected wire-endpoint warnings, two local/global name warnings and three off-grid endpoints. It has zero ERC errors. They are reported rather than suppressed or confused with PCB completion. We did not redraw Andrew's schematic to remove those warnings, change his historical circuit choices, or qualify hardware operation, thermal behaviour or signal integrity.

== Evidence and reproduction

The link:../reports/firstz80/verification.json[combined verification] checks all three native results against the frozen checkpoint: footprint/pad geometry, values, library identities, outline, layer count, all 371 net assignments, track/via dimensions, project hashes and fresh DRC/parity/connectivity. It also checks that no historical track/via UUID was reused. The earlier completed boards remain verified by our original schematic checker.

Each layout has a `routing.json` manifest. Trial inputs, route orders, failures, native findings and accepted candidates live under `reports/firstz80/`. The pure eight-direction failures remain distinguishable from the successful seed and refinement. Runtime caches and owned server profiles are ignored by Git; the official local MCP server has stopped.

Use `/usr/bin/python3 tools/check_firstz80.py` to recheck the published boards. `tools/prepare_firstz80.py` creates the copper-free input and portable legacy library snapshot once and refuses to overwrite an existing checkpoint. `tools/route_firstz80.py` preserves each trial separately; `tools/refine_firstz80_octilinear.py` is the explicit hybrid refinement. The FreeRouting driver and importer retain requests, responses and native corrections.

These tools are experiments worth improving. The key lesson from this denser two-layer case is that completion, topology, pad access and native validation matter more than a pleasing track angle or a router's own completion report. The earlier low-via four-layer result does not predict this board's result.
