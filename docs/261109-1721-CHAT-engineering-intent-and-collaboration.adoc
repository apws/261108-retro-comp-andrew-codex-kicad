== 3. Ari: engineering delegation

Yes, Falken 🙂 I’d describe our approach as *engineering delegation*:

[quote]
____
*Tell the assistant what result you need, provide the relevant sources and important constraints, and let it work out the implementation. Review the result together and steer where necessary.*
____

For Andrew, our session could be summarized like this:

[quote]
____
“Inspect the previous Grok/AGY work and the original schematic. Create your own KiCad version, then compare several routing approaches. Preserve the alternatives, check the results and document what you did.”
____

You didn’t have to specify the Python architecture, routing algorithms, export commands or validation scripts. I investigated and selected those as the work developed.

Your engineering input remained valuable: *Eurocard dimensions, a single A3 sheet, preferred component placement, readable wiring, and which experiments mattered.* An annotated phone photo of a rough layout can also communicate that intent; uncertain dimensions or connections still need clarification.

Detailed instructions are useful when they express a real requirement or hard-earned knowledge. There is usually no need to prescribe every operation merely to get the work started. This agrees with https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/prompting#prompting-codex[OpenAI’s prompting guidance], which emphasizes the desired behaviour, context, constraints and verification.

I’d call my role a *technical collaborator*. You provide purpose and engineering judgment; I contribute investigation, implementation and checks. Both contributions remain reviewable—and both can contain mistakes. Our schematic cleanup was a practical example of catching and correcting them together 🙂

== 6. Ari: small, self-contained and testable steps

Yes, Falken 🙂 *Small, self-contained, testable steps describe our workflow well.* Your “punk style” is compatible with that.

You can think aloud, add context, make jokes and change direction. My job is to extract the request, track the constraints and clarify consequential ambiguities. If something is essential, a simple *“must keep…”* helps make it unmistakable.

Our steps were understandable outcomes: create the schematic, compare routing variants, make a wired drawing, then review the human corrections. Each produced something you could inspect. You didn’t need to prescribe the implementation.
