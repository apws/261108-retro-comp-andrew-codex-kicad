== The "Weird" Sawtooth Pattern on B.Cu: Why It Occurs

When inspecting the bottom layer (`B.Cu`) between the peripheral header pins (`J2`–`J4`),
a distinctive serrated or "sawtooth" track pattern is visible:

image::images/261106-21 bottom pcb sawtooth clearance hug -- Screenshot_20261106_2139.png[Sawtooth clearance hug between header pins on B.Cu,width=500]

=== 1. Why does the router create this shape?
* **Circular Keepout Boundaries**: Around every through-hole pad, KiCad enforces a circular keepout envelope:
  `keepout_radius = pad_radius + clearance + (track_width / 2)`.
  For standard header pins (0.85 mm radius) with 0.15 mm clearance and 0.15 mm track width, this creates a circular exclusion zone.
* **Discrete 0.10 mm Orthogonal Grid**: The router (`tools/route_netgroup.py`) computes paths on a 0.10 mm discrete grid. When a continuous mathematical circle is projected onto a square grid, its boundary steps by exactly 1 grid cell (0.1 mm) at discrete intervals, forming a rasterized staircase.
* **Euclidean Shortest-Path Hugging**: The A* pathfinder minimizes total path length. Squeezing a vertical track between two closely spaced header pins, the mathematically shortest legal path is to ride directly against the circular clearance boundary. Following the rasterized circle step-for-step produces the visible sawtooth wave.
* **Manhattan Layer Biasing**: `B.Cu` is biased for vertical travel (penalty 1.0 for vertical, 3.0 for horizontal). The router prefers to stay on `B.Cu` rather than punch two vias to hop to `F.Cu` (via cost 8.0). It therefore negotiates the narrow bottleneck on `B.Cu` by hugging the pad clearance boundary.

=== 2. Is it safe and reliable for manufacturing?
* **Electrically Sound**: The track maintains uniform 0.15 mm width and strictly satisfies the $\ge 0.15$ mm clearance from the adjacent pad rings and vias. Because the 4-layer stack includes a solid +5V plane on `In2.Cu` immediately above `B.Cu` (~0.2 mm prepreg dielectric), return current loops are tightly coupled. At 4 MHz – 8 MHz Z80 signal frequencies, 0.1 mm jogs have zero measurable signal integrity impact.
* **DFM / Etching**: Modern PCB fabricators (such as JLCPCB) use laser direct imaging (LDI) and automated optical inspection (AOI). The 0.15 mm tracks and clearances fully satisfy JLCPCB standard 4-layer capabilities without acid trap or etching concerns.
* **DRC Verified**: Passes KiCad design rule checking with **0 errors, 0 shorts, and 0 clearance violations**.
